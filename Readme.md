## Overview
This project utilizes multi-temporal LIDAR to quantify wildfire related impacts on forest and topographical within a portion of the 2020 Beachie Creek fire. By comparing pre‑fire and post‑fire datasets, the analysis evaluates canopy loss, surviving vegetation structure, debris accumulation, and topographic stability following a high‑severity wildfire event.

## Data
**2008 USGS 3DEP LiDAR** <br>
Baseline pre‑fire dataset representing forest and terrain conditions prior to the Beachie Creek Fire.

**2022 USGS Western Wildfire Program LiDAR** <br>
Post‑fire dataset acquired after the burn, used to assess structural change and recovery patterns.

Both datasets provide sufficient point density and classification detail for ground modeling, canopy height analysis, and change detection.

## Study Area
**Location:** Santiam Canyon, Marion County, Oregon, US <br>
**Description:** A small 160 acre was chosen at random in the coverage zones of available LIDAR data. This area contains a ravine with a stream (fish creek) in the center, to the east and west are rising slopes. There are areas in the center and southeast portions of the study area that were previoulsy cleared prior to the 2008 USGS scans.
